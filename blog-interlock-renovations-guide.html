Interlock Driveways & Patios
in Ottawa: Everything You
Need to Know

Interlock March 10, 2025 By AMA United Company

If you're considering replacing your cracked concrete driveway or upgrading a dull asphalt surface, interlock paving is almost certainly the best decision for an Ottawa property. It looks better, lasts longer in our climate, and offers a flexibility that no monolithic surface can match. But getting the most out of interlock requires understanding how it works, what makes a quality installation, and what to watch for when choosing a contractor.

This is the complete guide to interlock driveways and patios in Ottawa — written by the crew that installs them every week across Barrhaven, Kanata, Nepean, Orléans, Manotick, and beyond.

What Is Interlock Paving?

Interlock paving — also called interlocking concrete pavers or ICP — uses individual concrete paving stones laid over a compacted granular base. The stones are precisely sized so they fit together tightly, with polymeric sand filling the joints. The resulting surface distributes load across multiple stones rather than concentrating stress in one slab, which is the fundamental reason interlock outperforms poured concrete and asphalt in freeze-thaw climates like Ottawa's.

Modern interlock pavers are manufactured under precise conditions and are available in a vast range of sizes, profiles, colours, and textures — from smooth contemporary formats to tumbled natural stone looks. The design flexibility of interlock far exceeds anything possible with poured concrete or asphalt.

Why Interlock Outperforms Concrete and Asphalt in Ottawa

Ottawa's climate creates a specific and predictable failure mode for both concrete and asphalt: the freeze-thaw cycle. When water infiltrates a hairline crack in poured concrete, freezes, and expands, it forces that crack wider. Repeat this process dozens of times per year — as Ottawa's climate does — and within 5–10 years you have a surface covered in expanding cracks, heaved sections, and crumbling edges. Asphalt fares somewhat better due to its flexibility, but it softens in Ottawa's summer heat, develops ruts under vehicle loads, and still cracks and pothole over time.

Interlock handles this cycle completely differently:

  • No monolithic slab to crack: Because interlock is composed of individual stones, there is no continuous slab that can develop a propagating crack. Each stone is a discrete unit.
  • Flexible jointing: The polymeric sand joints between stones accommodate minor ground movement without the stones cracking or separating in a damaging way.
  • Repairability: If a section heaves due to a utility repair, tree root intrusion, or extreme frost, individual stones are lifted, the base is re-graded, and the stones are relaid. No saw cutting, no patching, no permanent colour mismatch — just the original stones back in place.
  • Drainage: Properly installed interlock allows water to drain through the joints and into the granular base, reducing surface water pooling and ice formation in winter.

Design Options: Patterns, Colours, and Textures

One of interlock's greatest strengths is design flexibility. The major decisions are:

Stone Profile

Rectangular pavers are the most versatile and can be laid in herringbone, running bond, basket weave, or soldier course patterns. Tumbled pavers have broken edges and a worn appearance that mimics natural cobblestone — excellent for traditional and heritage-style homes. Large-format pavers (18"×18" and larger) create a contemporary, clean look well-suited to modern homes. Flagstone-look pavers replicate irregular natural stone shapes in a manufactured format that installs more consistently.

Laying Patterns

The most popular patterns for Ottawa driveways and patios include herringbone (the strongest pattern for driveways — the interlocking angle distributes vehicle loads extremely well), running bond (clean and contemporary), and basketweave (classic and traditional). Borders in contrasting colours and different stone orientations add definition and visual interest.

Colour Selection

Interlock pavers are available in natural concrete greys, warm beiges and buffs, rich charcoals, reds, and blended multi-colour options that replicate the variation of natural stone. The most durable colours are integral — meaning the pigment runs through the entire stone, not just the surface — so they don't fade or chip off over decades of use.

The Installation Process: What a Quality Job Looks Like

The quality of an interlock installation is 80% about what you can't see after the job is done — the base preparation. This is where inexperienced or budget-cutting contractors consistently cut corners, and where the difference between an interlock surface that lasts 30 years and one that heaves in three years is determined.

A properly installed Ottawa interlock driveway follows this sequence:

  1. Demolition and removal: Existing concrete, asphalt, or old interlock is broken up and hauled away completely.
  2. Excavation: The subgrade is excavated to the required depth — typically 10–12 inches for driveways, 8–10 inches for patios. The depth must accommodate the granular base, bedding sand, and paver thickness.
  3. Subgrade compaction: The native soil subgrade is compacted with a plate compactor. Soft or unstable subgrade areas are identified and addressed — this is a critical step that's easy to skip.
  4. Granular base installation: Crushed stone (typically Granular A or equivalent) is placed in lifts and compacted to the specified depth. This is the most important step in the entire installation. A base that is too thin, uncompacted, or uses the wrong material will fail in Ottawa's freeze-thaw climate regardless of the quality of stones on top.
  5. Bedding sand: A 1-inch layer of coarse bedding sand is screeded to a precise, consistent grade. This provides a levelling bed for the pavers and allows minor adjustments during laying.
  6. Paver installation: Stones are laid in the specified pattern, cut cleanly at borders with a masonry saw, and checked constantly for level and pattern consistency.
  7. Edge restraints: Plastic or aluminum edge restraints are spiked into the base along all free edges to prevent lateral movement over time.
  8. Compaction and joint sand: The completed surface is compacted with a plate compactor to set the stones into the bedding sand. Polymeric sand is swept into the joints and activated with water — it hardens to prevent joint erosion and inhibit weed growth.

Ask any contractor you're evaluating to walk you through this sequence in detail. Vague answers about the base preparation process are a red flag.

Ottawa Climate Considerations

A few Ottawa-specific points worth understanding:

  • Winter maintenance: Interlock driveways can be plowed and salted like any other surface. Polymeric sand is formulated to resist salt damage. Metal plow blades should be fitted with rubber or poly shoes to prevent gouging the paver surface.
  • Spring assessment: The best time to check your interlock surface is in spring, after the frost has fully come out of the ground. Minor settlement in the first 1–2 seasons after installation is normal and expected as the base fully consolidates.
  • Tree roots: Mature trees near a driveway or patio can eventually lift paver sections as roots grow. Interlock handles this far better than concrete — affected sections can be lifted, roots addressed, and stones relaid without visible repair evidence.

Maintenance: What to Expect

Interlock is not zero-maintenance, but it is low-maintenance when properly installed:

  • Annual inspection: Walk your interlock surface in spring and fall. Look for settled or raised stones, joint sand loss, and edge restraint integrity.
  • Joint sand top-up: Polymeric sand in the joints may erode slightly over 3–5 years, especially in high-traffic areas. Sweeping in additional polymeric sand and reactivating with water is a straightforward repair.
  • Weed management: Quality polymeric sand inhibits weed growth significantly, but is not 100% weed-proof over time. Occasional spot treatment with a suitable herbicide keeps joints clean.
  • Sealing: Applying a penetrating interlock sealer every 3–5 years enhances colour, hardens the joint sand further, and provides additional stain resistance. It's optional but recommended for driveways.

Cost: What to Budget for Interlock in Ottawa (2025)

Interlock costs in Ottawa vary based on square footage, stone selection, pattern complexity, and site conditions. Rough 2025 price ranges for installed interlock:

  • Standard interlock driveway (single car): $8,000–$14,000
  • Standard interlock driveway (double car): $14,000–$24,000
  • Interlock patio (200–300 sq ft): $6,000–$12,000
  • Front walkway: $2,500–$6,000
  • Retaining wall (per linear foot): $150–$300+

Premium stone selections, complex patterns, significant demolition, or difficult site access add to these ranges. Always obtain a detailed written quote that specifies the base depth, materials, and scope of work — not a per-square-foot ballpark.

Choosing the Right Interlock Contractor in Ottawa

The quality of your interlock installation is almost entirely determined by the contractor you choose. The stones themselves are a commodity — the base preparation, compaction quality, and attention to detail during installation are what separate a 30-year surface from a 5-year problem.

Key things to look for:

  • Specific experience with Ottawa interlock installations — ask about base depth specifications and compaction methods.
  • A willingness to explain the installation process in detail before any money changes hands.
  • A detailed written quote that specifies base depth, materials, stone brand and series, pattern, and scope of demolition.
  • References from completed Ottawa interlock projects you can verify.
  • A written workmanship warranty.
