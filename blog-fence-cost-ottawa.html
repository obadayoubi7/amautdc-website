How Much Does a
Fence Cost
in Ottawa? (2026 Prices)

Fencing August 2, 2026 By AMA United Company

"How much does a fence cost?" is the very first question almost every Ottawa homeowner asks — and the honest answer is "it depends," which isn't very useful on its own. This guide breaks that answer down into real numbers: 2026 price ranges by material, the specific factors that push a quote up or down, and what a fair, detailed estimate should actually include.

These figures reflect what Ottawa homeowners are actually paying for professionally installed fencing this year. If you want the deeper dive on which material fits your property, see our complete residential fencing guide — this post focuses specifically on the numbers.

What Determines Fence Cost in Ottawa

Every fence quote is built from the same handful of variables. Understanding them helps you read a quote critically instead of just comparing a single bottom-line number between contractors:

  • Material: The single biggest driver of cost. Chain link is the most affordable; ornamental iron and steel are the most expensive.
  • Length: Fencing is priced per linear foot, so total property perimeter (or the section you're fencing) scales the total directly.
  • Height: A 6-foot privacy fence costs more per foot than a 4-foot fence — more material and more labour per post.
  • Gates: Priced separately from the linear-foot rate, since hardware and framing vary by size and style.
  • Site conditions: Slopes, corners, tree roots, rocky soil, and difficult equipment access all add labour time.
  • Removal of an old fence: Demolition and disposal of an existing fence is additional scope if you're replacing rather than installing new.

2026 Price Ranges by Material

These are installed prices for a standard 6-foot residential privacy fence, including posts, concrete, labour, and cleanup:

  • Chain Link (galvanized, 4'): starting from $40 per linear foot, typically $40–$55
  • Pressure-Treated Wood (6'): $55–$85 per linear foot
  • Cedar Wood (6'): $65–$95 per linear foot
  • PVC/Vinyl Privacy (6'): starting from $75 per linear foot, typically $75–$110
  • Hybrid Fencing (6'): $90–$130 per linear foot
  • Ornamental Iron/Steel: $120–$200+ per linear foot

Gates add $300–$800+ each depending on width, style, and hardware. These ranges assume reasonably flat, accessible terrain — expect the upper end (or above it) for challenging sites.

Real-World Cost Examples

To make these numbers concrete, here's what a typical 150-linear-foot backyard fence — a common perimeter length for an Ottawa suburban lot — looks like across materials:

  • Chain link (150 ft): roughly $6,000–$8,250
  • Pressure-treated wood (150 ft): roughly $8,250–$12,750
  • PVC privacy (150 ft): roughly $11,250–$16,500
  • Hybrid (150 ft): roughly $13,500–$19,500

Add a gate or two, and factor in any grade changes or extra corners, and you'll land within — or just past — those ranges. A shorter side-yard run or a longer full-perimeter fence scales proportionally.

Hidden Costs to Watch For

The line items that catch homeowners off guard usually aren't the fence itself — they're the details that get left off a vague quote:

  • Old fence removal: Confirm whether demolition and disposal of your existing fence is included or billed separately.
  • Gates: Always priced per gate, not folded into the linear-foot rate.
  • Extra posts on corners and slopes: A yard with many angles needs more posts per foot than a straight run.
  • Utility locates: Reputable contractors arrange a utility locate before digging — this protects you but should be part of the process, not an add-on charge.

Permits are rarely a cost factor for standard backyard fencing — most fences under 2 metres (6.5 ft) don't require one from the City of Ottawa, though corner lots and taller fences can be exceptions.

How to Get an Accurate Quote

Ballpark phone quotes are unreliable because so much depends on your specific property. A trustworthy Ottawa fencing contractor will:

  • Visit your property in person to measure and assess site conditions.
  • Provide a detailed, itemized written quote — not a single vague total.
  • Specify material, height, post spacing, and whether old-fence removal and gates are included.

Reducing Cost Without Sacrificing Quality

If your quote comes in higher than expected, there are legitimate ways to bring the number down without cutting corners on installation quality:

  • Fence only what needs fencing. If part of your property line borders a neighbour's existing fence in good condition, or a natural barrier like dense hedging, you may not need to fence that section at all.
  • Choose a shorter height where bylaws and privacy needs allow. A 4-foot fence uses less material and less labour per foot than a 6-foot privacy fence.
  • Simplify the gate count and style. Each additional gate is its own line item — a single well-placed gate is often enough for most backyards.
  • Compare materials on total cost of ownership, not just sticker price. Chain link looks cheapest upfront, but if your goal is privacy, a mid-range wood fence may actually cost less than adding privacy slats to chain link later.

What you shouldn't cut to save money: post depth, concrete quality, or hardware grade. These are exactly the details that separate a fence that lasts 20 years from one that needs repair in five, and they're a small fraction of the total project cost.
